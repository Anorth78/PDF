Strategic Visions and AgenticOps 
Why did Cisco build Cisco Cloud Control? 
Cisco Cloud Control is Cisco’s strategic response to one of enterprise IT’s most persistent 
challenges: fragmented operations across disconnected management planes. 
Customers manage increasingly complex environments spanning networking, security, 
observability, collaboration, compute, and cloud. Operational teams often have to move 
between multiple consoles to understand inventory, correlate events, troubleshoot 
incidents, enforce policy, and demonstrate compliance. This fragmentation slows 
response, increases operational risk, creates inconsistent visibility, and makes it harder for 
IT teams to deliver predictable outcomes. 
Cisco Cloud Control establishes a unified operational layer across Cisco’s portfolio. It 
provides a single starting point for visibility, insights, workflows, and agentic operations 
while preserving customers’ investments in the purpose-built controllers used within each 
domain. By connecting portfolio context, identity, data, and workflows, Cloud Control

helps customers reduce complexity, improve time to insight and resolution, and establish a 
governed foundation for agentic operations. 
Why Cisco? What makes us different in comparison to our competitors? 
Cisco brings decades of product depth across networking, security, compute, 
observability, and collaboration. Cisco Cloud Control connects that depth so products can 
contribute context, intelligence, and action to a broader operational environment. 
Each product retains its specialized capabilities and management experience. Cisco Cloud 
Control adds the shared foundation needed to work across them. Supported partner and 
third-party systems extend that environment without requiring customers to replace the 
tools that already meet their needs. 
What makes it different is: 
• Portfolio depth becomes a cross-domain advantage: Cisco Cloud Control connects 
context and action across networking, security, compute, observability, and 
collaboration, helping teams understand dependencies, find cause and impact 
faster, and resolve issues that span IT domains. 
• The foundation is connected, not just the interface: Shared identity, inventory, 
topology, telemetry, policy, and action create a consistent operational foundation. 
Products, apps, and agents can build on it without recreating core services or 
operational context. 
• Product depth is preserved: Teams keep the specialized capabilities and 
management experiences they rely on while gaining shared context across 
products. 
• It is built for teams and AI agents: Operators and agents work from the same trusted 
context, permissions, and tools, helping teams investigate faster, reduce handoff 
friction, and act with appropriate oversight. 
• It adapts to the customer: Organizations can build custom apps and agents and 
connect supported third-party systems, adding capabilities around their needs 
without replacing existing tools and investments. 
What does "platform" mean in the context of Cisco Cloud Control? 
Cisco Cloud Control is more than a navigation portal.

How is AgenticOps different from traditional AIOps? 
Traditional AIOps focuses on detection and insight with AI to correlate events, reduce 
noise, and surface recommendations. In practice, it often stops at alerts or suggested 
actions, which leaves teams to manually coordinate next steps across tools and domains. 
AgenticOps goes further by coordination of the full operational workflow. AI agents actively 
gather evidence, maintain context, and help drive work end-to-end—from investigation 
through approved action and validation, while humans retain control at all times. The result 
is faster, more continuous operations with governance, accountability, and auditability 
built in. 
How do Cisco Cloud Control and AI Canvas fit together? 
Think of Cloud Control as the harness and AI Canvas as the situation room inside it. Cloud 
Control provides the foundation every team works from with one login, unified inventory, 
shared topology, correlated alerts, and governed identity. AI Canvas is where agents 
operate: a collaborative workspace where operators and AI agents investigate and resolve 
cross-domain issues together. You always start in Cloud Control. When an issue needs 
deeper investigation or team collaboration, you open Canvas and it inherits all of Cloud 
Control's context automatically. Cloud Control without Canvas is a unified platform. 
Canvas without Cloud Control is a clever workspace. Together, they're an operating agentic 
workspace. 
Is AI Canvas a standalone product? 
No, AI Canvas is not a standalone product. It is an experience within Cisco Cloud Control.

What is AI Canvas? 
AI Canvas is the multiplayer, agentic workspace within Cisco Cloud Control where 
operators and AI agents investigate, decide, and act together. It keeps evidence and 
context in one place so teams can find cause and impact faster, continue across handoffs 
without starting over, and move from investigation to approved action. 
What key pain points does AI Canvas solve? 
• Investigations are scattered. Evidence, findings, decisions, and actions are spread 
across different tools and conversations. 
• Cause and impact stay hidden. Individual products may show symptoms without 
revealing dependencies across IT domains. 
• Handoffs reset the work. Teams lose investigation history when an issue moves 
between operators, shifts, or specialized teams. 
• Action stalls after investigation. Teams lack a clear path from findings and 
recommendations to approved action. 
What are the key, high-level features of AI Canvas? 
• Multiplayer workspace: One live environment where operators and AI agents work 
together from the same evidence and operational context. 
• Cross-domain investigation: Agent-led analysis and correlation across products and 
IT domains. 
• Persistent context: A durable record of evidence, findings, decisions, and actions 
across sessions and handoffs. 
• Governed action: A controlled path for reviewing recommendations, approving next 
steps, and carrying out authorized actions. 
What are the key outcomes to expect from AI Canvas? 
• Bring investigations together. Spend less time switching tools, gathering evidence, 
and coordinating separate conversations. 
• Find cause and impact faster. Reveal relationships across products and IT domains 
that individual tools may not show. 
• Keep work moving through handoffs. Continue across teams and shifts without 
repeating work or rebuilding the investigation.

• Reach resolution with control. Move from findings to approved actions while 
keeping operators involved in important decisions. 
What does it mean that AI Canvas is a "multiplayer" workspace? 
AI Canvas provides a shared, synchronized workspace where operators and AI agents can 
work from the same evidence, findings, notes, and investigation history. The workspace 
preserves context across collaboration and handoffs so another operator can continue the 
investigation without reconstructing the work from the beginning. 
What are widgets in AI Canvas and how are they organized? 
Widgets are the visual building blocks on the AI Canvas workspace. Each widget represents 
a specific piece of operational information such as a network overview, a device detail 
view, a topology map, a health assessment, or a compliance report. Widgets are color-
coded by type (for example, networks, topologies, health assessments, security) to help 
operators quickly identify and organize them. Widgets can be collapsed, stacked by 
category, extracted from stacks, resized to full screen, and arranged on the canvas. 
Operators choose which items to promote to widgets on the canvas with a plus-sign 
control, in order to keep the workspace focused on what matters for the current 
investigation rather than automatic population of it with every query result. 
Can customers bring their own knowledge documents into AI Canvas? 
Yes. AI Canvas supports customer-uploaded knowledge sources that can be referenced 
during investigations. Customers can upload documents such as network contact lists, 
outage histories, SLA details, ISP information, or operational runbooks. These documents 
are then available to be pulled into any prompt using a hashtag command, which allows 
the AI to incorporate the customer's own organizational context when it answers questions 
or during performance analysis. 
What is deep thinking mode in AI Canvas? 
Deep thinking is an investigation mode in AI Canvas that performs a thorough, multi-step 
analysis of a problem. When invoked, it creates a dedicated investigation environment, 
builds an execution plan, gathers evidence across available data sources, writes and runs 
analytical code, and produces a comprehensive report which includes primary cause, 
contributing factors, impact assessment, and an evidence trail. Deep thinking takes longer 
than a quick analysis (typically a few minutes) but provides significantly more depth and 
rigor. Operators can choose between deep reason and quick analysis based on the 
complexity of the issue. 
Does AI Canvas support multimodal input such as images?

--- PAGE 8 ---
AI Canvas includes a vision analysis agent that can interpret images. Operators can 
capture a screenshot, such as a topology map or a dashboard view, and drop it into the AI 
Canvas chat. The vision analysis agent inventories the contents of the image, creates 
metadata, compares it with known network context, and provides an analysis. For 
example, an operator could screenshot a topology and ask "Do we have any problems in 
this network?" and receive an informed response based on both the image and live 
platform data. 
Does AI Canvas generate downloadable reports and artifacts? 
Yes. AI Canvas supports artifacts downloads such as topology maps as PDFs, compliance 
audit reports, and other investigation outputs. Operators can export these for 
documentation, share with stakeholders, or offline review.

Yes. Cisco Cloud Control includes a Unified AI Assistant—a global, natural language

When should I tell my customer to use AI Assistant vs. AI Canvas? 
Use AI Assistant for quick, single-player questions, such as "What's the health of my 
Chicago branch?" or "Show me devices approaching end-of-support. " Use AI Canvas for 
deeper, multi-step, or multi-team investigations, such as "Why is our finance app slow 
today, and what do we do about it?" If an operator starts in Assistant and realizes they need 
more, one click escalates into Canvas with full context preserved. Assistant is for seconds-
to-minutes conversations while Canvas is for minutes-to-hours investigations. Both use 
the same reasoning engine, so answers stay consistent regardless of where the question is 
asked.

Agent Builder in Cloud Control Studio 
What is Agent Builder? 
Agent Builder is the second builder environment inside Cloud Control Studio. It lets 
customers build customer agents in natural language tailored to their own policies and 
workflows. Customers can connect agents to third-party tools and create custom agent 
skills from operational knowledge resources (like SOPs or runbooks). 
How do agents that are built in Agent Builder run in Cisco Cloud Control? 
Agents built in Cloud Control Studio run in one of two modes, chosen by the publisher 
when the agent is configured. 
Ambient agents run continuously in Cisco Cloud Control. They operate in the background, 
monitoring data, watching for conditions, and acting or alerting when something happens. 
Ambient agents run on their own schedule or in response to events. Operators don't need 
to be in a session for them to work and will be notified if the agent requires input or 
approval from them. Live agents are invoked during sessions in Cisco AI Canvas. They work 
alongside operators in real time on active investigations. Live agents respond to the work 
happening in the session and exit when the work is done. 
Who builds agents in the Agent Builder? 
Studio is for Cisco Cloud Control users including IT admins, network operations teams, and 
partners who need custom workflows, dashboards, agents, operational apps, or action-
oriented experiences that reflect their business environment, without requiring them to 
write code. 
The intent is that the people closest to the operational problem, not specialized AI 
engineers, build the agents that solve it. A network operator who knows the right way to 
triage a Wi-Fi outage can encode that knowledge into an agent. A SecOps lead who knows 
their organization's incident response sequence can build that into an ambient agent.

A team building on LangChain might spend months assembling all of this before their first

What is an "agent skill" in Agent Builder? 
A skill is a piece of operational logic an AI agent can call, such as a runbook procedure, a 
remediation step, a diagnostic check, or a policy validation. Skills can be simple (run a 
specific check, return a status) or complex (execute a multi-step procedure with 
conditional logic). 
Skills are reusable across agents. Encoding a procedure once makes it available to every 
agent that needs it. A skill that captures the customer's standard approach to firewall rule 
validation can be called by a security agent, a compliance agent, and a network change 
agent. The same logic, called from different agents in different contexts. 
Skills are managed in Cloud Control Studio with version control. As the customer's 
operational practices evolve, skills are updated centrally, and every agent that uses them 
inherits the change. 
How are agents built in Agent Builder monitored and evaluated? 
Every agent run, trace, failure, and quality score is powered by Splunk AI Agent Monitoring 
and Galileo, both included with Cisco Cloud Control Studio. No separate tools or 
subscriptions required. 
Splunk AI Agent Monitoring captures execution data: every agent run, with outcome 
(success, warning, failed), duration, and trigger source. Builders can drill into traces 
showing each LLM call, tool invocation, and skill execution, with inputs and outputs. 
Failures (error codes, authorization issues, tool timeouts) surface in context on the agent's 
detail page. 
Splunk AI Agent Monitoring (via Galileo) evaluates output quality: it scores agent outputs 
for accuracy, relevance, and correctness using LLM-as-a-judge methodology. It flags 
hallucinations, where agents fabricate information or cite unsupported data. It also detects 
drift, alerting builders when an agent's quality degrades over time. 
Together, observability and evaluation give builders a closed loop: see what agents did, see 
how well they did it, and improve them. 
How are agents built in Agent Builder governed and controlled? 
Every agent runs under policy enforcement through Cisco AI Defense at both build time 
and runtime. Customers define what each agent can do, when it can act, and what actions 
require human approval. Agents propose actions; humans approve before anything 
changes on production.

How are agents tested before they go live in Agent Builder? 
Agents go through a draft, sandbox, and publish lifecycle before they can act on 
production. 
Draft. The agent is being built. The builder describes what it should do, configures its tools 
and skills, and iterates on its logic. Sandbox. The agent runs against real data in a sandbox 
environment. Builders can simulate scenarios, observe the agent's behavior, review traces, 
and verify that outputs meet quality expectations. Publish. Once the builder is satisfied, the 
agent is published with a version number. Publishing requires an approval gate. From there, 
the agent is available to run in its configured mode (ambient or live). 
Version control tracks every change. Customers can roll back to a prior version if a new 
version causes issues, and full execution history is preserved across versions for auditing 
and comparison.

How is Agent Builder different from a workflow automation tool? 
Workflow automation tools execute predefined steps. Given a trigger, they run a script that 
has been written in advance. The logic is static. If conditions change, the script either fails 
or produces the wrong result. 
Cisco Cloud Control Studio builds AI agents that reason across data, decide what to do, 
and propose actions to humans for approval. The work is dynamic and context-aware, not 
script-based. 
A concrete example: a workflow automation tool can be configured to restart a service 
when CPU exceeds a threshold. An AI agent can investigate why CPU is high, correlate it 
with recent deployments, network conditions, and security events, identify the root cause, 
and propose either a service restart or a different remediation depending on what the data 
actually shows. The agent reasons about the situation. The workflow tool just runs. 
Agents and workflows are complementary. Workflows are still the right answer for fully 
predictable, low-variance tasks. Agents are the right answer when the work requires 
judgment, cross-domain context, or adaptation to conditions that weren't known when the 
logic was written.

What is Cisco Cloud Control not? 
Cloud Control is not an ITSM platform and it doesn't replace ServiceNow or Jira. It is not a 
SIEM and it doesn't replace Splunk. It is not a SOAR tool, although it complements one. It is 
not a replacement for individual Cisco product controllers and those remain fully 
functional. It is the operations layer that unifies and adds agentic intelligence on top of all 
of these. 
Is Cisco Cloud Control just a redesigned UI or another portal on top of Cisco's current 
tools? 
No. While Cloud Control provides a modern, unified navigation experience, it also includes 
platform-layer services such as unified identity, normalized inventory, an AI Assistant that 
provides consistent, context-aware guidance across every domain, and AI Canvas. Cisco 
Cloud Control therefore lets you operate as if the tools were one system, a fundamentally 
different category. See "What does "platform" mean in the context of Cisco Cloud 
Control?" for more background. 
How does Cisco Cloud Control get data from the individual products? 
Cloud Control uses a combination of push and pull mechanisms based on the product. 
Some products push data proactively to Cloud Control, while others require Cloud Control 
to pull data via APIs. The platform uses Cisco Unified Identity-based tokens to authenticate 
and authorize these data exchanges. In order to continually reduce time to insight and 
resolution, the goal is for all data to flow as close to real-time as possible and to enable 
meaningful cross-domain correlation. 
How does Cisco Cloud Control keep inventory and operational data current? 
Synchronization varies by source and generally occurs from every few minutes to once an 
hour. Most products push data to Cloud Control. If connectivity is lost, Cloud Control 
retains the last-synchronized inventory but cannot update it until connectivity returns. 
Decommissioned devices must be removed from the source controller before they 
disappear from Cloud Control.

can move across integrated Cisco products in Cloud Control without repeated logins, while

What is tenant linking and why does it matter to my customer? 
Most customers have multiple Cisco product tenants, such as one for Meraki, one for 
Security Cloud Control, one for ThousandEyes, and so on. Tenant linking is how an 
administrator connects those tenants into a single unified Cloud Control experience. An 
admin does this once. From that point forward, every user in the organization gets single 
sign-on, one unified inventory, one topology, and one alerting view across every linked 
product without each user configuring anything individually. It's the foundational step that 
turns a collection of Cisco products into a unified operations environment.

File a TAC case from within Cisco Cloud Control. TAC will coordinate with engineering to 
change or remove the existing tenant-group linking.

How does Cisco Cloud Control’s data enhance AI Canvas? 
Cloud Control aggregates and normalizes inventory, topology, and telemetry from across 
Cisco's portfolio. This data is exposed to AI Canvas through Model Context Protocol 
services, which gives AI Canvas rich, cross-domain context about every asset in a 
customer's environment which includes other devices, relationships, and topology. This 
means when AI Canvas investigates an issue, it already understands the full operational 
landscape, which enables faster and more accurate cross-domain reason without the 
requirement of operators to manually provide context. 
Does every connected controller expose the same inventory, advisory, topology, and 
action data? 
No. Data depth and supported actions vary by controller, product release, entitlement, and 
integration. Cloud Control can unify the available information, but it cannot expose data or 
actions that the source system does not provide. 
What do you mean by "zero ingestion overhead"?

--- PAGE 22 ---
AI Canvas doesn't copy or store your live operational data. When it needs to answer a 
question like "what's happening on this switch right now?" , it queries the source system in 
real time and generates the result when the investigation ends. That means no duplicated 
data, no extra storage costs, and no lag. The exception is inventory and topology, which 
Cloud Control does normalize and store at the platform level because that data is most 
valuable when unified across domains. The live telemetry is pulled on demand; inventory 
and topology are always-on, always-unified. 
Does AI Canvas support push notifications from downstream applications? 
No. AI Canvas pulls data when needed, as directed by its AI agents. It leverages Model 
Context Protocol connections to supported platforms. AI Canvas does not passively collect 
or ingest data. 
What is Model Context Protocol (MCP) and why is it important? 
Model Context Protocol is a standards-based protocol that enables communication 
between AI agents and tools (like Meraki or Splunk). It creates an abstraction layer, which 
allows Cisco to rapidly onboard new products and expand the ecosystem of tools that can 
be integrated into AI Canvas without the design process of custom, proprietary APIs for 
each one. 
How do specialized Cloud Control agents interact with product MCP servers? 
Cisco Cloud Control defines specialized agents for particular operational scopes. 
Integrated products expose tools and endpoints through their Model Context Protocol 
servers, which the agents use to retrieve information and perform permitted operations. 
Are Cisco's Model Context Protocol servers open source? Can customers extend 
them? 
Currently, Cisco's official Model Context Protocol servers are not open source. However, 
there is strong interest from the field for reference servers that customers and partners can 
extend and customize. The Cisco DevNet team tracks both community and official servers 
at: developer.cisco.com/codeexchange/ai. Future plans around open extensibility are 
under evaluation. 
Will the Deep Network Model (DNM) be available on GitHub? 
No. The focus is on integrating DNM into AI Canvas to power troubleshooting and reasoning 
rather than providing direct model access.

How does role-based access control (RBAC) work? 
Cloud Control with AI Canvas honor each user's entitlements and permissions based on 
what is configured on the underlying integrated platforms (Meraki, Security Cloud Control, 
ThousandEyes, etc.). A user's ability to view data or execute changes is determined by their 
existing permissions in the source platforms. If a user lacks access to a data source, they 
will not be able to view the corresponding data within the workspace. Unless they are a full 
tenant admin... in which case they can see the data in the workspace but cannot open any 
of the products they are not part of. 
How does role-based access control work if an administrator has different permission 
levels across different Cisco platforms? 
Role-based access control is respected across the board. For example, if you enter a 
colleague's shared workspace and they have widgets for a platform you don't have access 
to (e.g., Nexus Dashboard), you will not be able to view the data for those widgets. Your 
access level is always governed by the source platform's permissions. 
Exception for full tenant administrators: If you are a full tenant administrator, you can see 
all data within the Cloud Control workspace. However, you cannot open or access any 
individual product dashboards (Meraki, Intersight, etc.) for which you do not have explicit 
permissions in that product. This ensures that administrative visibility within Cloud Control 
does not inadvertently grant access to underlying product consoles. 
For Read-only users: If you have read-only access, you will be able to view data across all 
products you have permissions for, but you cannot execute configurations. Your access 
level is always determined by your entitlements in the source platforms—Cloud Control 
enforces these permissions at all times.
